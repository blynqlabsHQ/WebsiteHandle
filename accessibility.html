Accessibility

This site is part of the product.

A company building for blind and low vision people should be judged by its own front door. This is the standard this one is held to.

Standard WCAG 2.2 AA Last reviewed September 2026

The standard

What every page does.

Screen readers
One heading outline, no skips.

One h1 per page, every section named, every image described, and a skip link as the first thing on the page. Decorative motion is hidden from assistive technology and its content is carried in text that every reader gets.

Keyboard
Everything reachable, nothing trapped.

Every control is a real link, button or input, with a visible focus ring. The mobile menu closes with Escape and returns focus to where you were. The glasses on the design page turn with the arrow keys.

Contrast
Measured, not eyeballed.

Text colours are chosen against the real background they sit on, to at least the AA ratio. The palette has no accent colour, so nothing depends on telling two hues apart.

Motion
The still page is the designed page.

Every page is built static first, and motion is layered on only when your system allows it. With reduced motion set, the product film is never downloaded and nothing moves on its own.

The simulator
You hear what the wearer hears.

On the how-it-works page, Blynq's output in the conversation simulator is announced through a polite live region. A screen reader hears exactly the cues the glasses would give, and nothing else. Sound is off until you turn it on.

Tell us

Something did not work for you?

Tell us what you were trying to do, the page you were on, and the setup you use: screen reader, browser, magnifier or braille display. It goes straight to the people who built the page.
