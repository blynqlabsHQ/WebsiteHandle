How it works

Most of what it tells you is not a sentence.

A conversation gives you no room for a paragraph. So it earns every word it spends.

Tier 0 · a tone Tier 1 · a word Tier 2 · an answer

The window

A conversation leaves about a fifth of a second.

Across ten languages, from major world languages to small indigenous communities, people begin their reply within about 200 milliseconds of the other person finishing. Stivers et al., “Universals and cultural variation in turn-taking in conversation”, PNAS, 2009

That is the whole space a device has to work in without talking over someone. So the fastest thing Blynq says is not a word at all.

Three tiers

A tone, a word, or a full answer.

Tier 0
under 200ms
No words at all.

Two rising notes mean someone joined the room. Learned in ten minutes, then free forever. This is most of what you hear.

Tier 1
in the gaps
One to four words.

“Someone on your left.” Spoken only into detected silence. It will not speak while a human is speaking, and it will drop the cue rather than interrupt.

Tier 2
on request
A full answer, and only if you asked.

One press on the temple. Nothing is ever spoken at you unprompted.
