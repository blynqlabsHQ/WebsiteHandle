How it works

Most of what it tells you is not a sentence.

A conversation gives you no room for a paragraph. So it earns every word it spends.

Tier 0
under 200ms
No words at all.

Two rising notes mean someone joined the room. Learned in ten minutes, then free forever. This is most of what you hear.

Tier 1
in the gaps
One to four words.

“Someone on your left.” Spoken only into detected silence. It will not speak while a human is speaking, and it will drop the cue rather than interrupt.

Tier 2
on request
A full answer, and only if you asked.

One press on the temple. Nothing is ever spoken at you unprompted.

Privacy

The room is heard, not kept.

The microphone is there for timing, not identity. It listens for when someone is speaking so the glasses know to stay out of it. No audio is kept and no faces are learned.

What you asked and what you were told stays on your phone. It is the only way to check what a device that speaks actually said.

These are worn in public, around people who did not choose to be near a device. Building for that is not a compliance exercise. It is the reason the product can be worn at all.

Design

Three things on the temple.

There is no display. Every response is spoken, which means nothing on your face can take you out of the room you are in.

Button

A single recessed button on the outer temple. It is the only way to ask for a full answer, which is why nothing is ever spoken at you unprompted.

Speaker

A grille set into the underside of the arm, aimed at your ear rather than into it. Your ear canal stays open, so the room stays audible and the real conversation still gets through.

Microphone

A pinhole at the hinge. It hears the room in order to know when someone is speaking, and stores no audio.

Who it is for

One channel. A lot of rooms.

  1. 01
    Blind and low vision adults

    Professionally active, screen reader fluent, already listening at three to five hundred words a minute. Built to their speed, not to a comfortable demo speed.

  2. 02
    Anyone in a room too loud, or in a second language

    A conference floor, a bar, a call in your third language. The signal everyone else reads for free is the first thing noise takes away.

  3. 03
    Neurodivergent users

    For anyone who would rather have the social signal stated than inferred, the same channel does the same job.

  4. 04
    Deafblind users, through a braille display

    The output layer is abstracted from the speaker. The same cues can arrive on a braille line instead of in your ear.
